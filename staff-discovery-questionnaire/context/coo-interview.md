# COO Discovery Interview — Context Document

Interview conducted 2026-08-19 (COO's third day on the job, started
2026-08-17). This document is the source context for the question sets in
`../index.html`. It contains internal business information only — no client
or member data.

## Business snapshot

- Profitable, but thin margins: **15–22% monthly**.
- ~**140 members**. Team: **9 trainers** (1099 contractors), **1 part-time
  front desk** employee, CEO, COO. One trainer (Josie) also flexes into an
  admin-assistant role and runs social content.
- Weekday rhythm: busy mornings; **very quiet from ~noon to 5pm**.
- **New facility ~15 minutes away, move-in around February 1.** Open
  question: how many clients move with us vs. get lost in the move.

## Why these interviews

The COO is new to the fitness industry and wants each role's perspective —
and the reasoning behind past decisions — before driving changes. Goals:
make the business more efficient while *raising* output quality, protect
what works, increase lead flow and brand awareness.

## The offer (from the Momentum Service Sheet)

"4 Tiers. 1 Pathway" — each tier is a graduation, not a discount:

1. **RX Protocol** — full muscle+medicine integration (metabolic, nervous
   system, cardiopulmonary testing, VO2 max, labs, DEXA, delivered meals,
   5x/week training). $9,600 per 3-month program (or 3 × $3,600).
2. **Prescriptive Training** — clinical 1:1 rebuild. Single session $175;
   2x/wk $1,120/mo ($140/session); 3x/wk $1,560/mo ($130); 4x/wk $2,120/mo
   ($120). +$19.99 league fee. No contract; 30-day cancellation notice.
3. **Hybrid Bridge** — transition from foundation to performance.
4. **Physics 101** — long-term readiness (group class).

Plus **Partner Training**: $160/session for two, minimum 4 sessions/month
autopay, memberships $39.99 + $9.99 (min $689.98/mo). Money-back standard
guarantee. Most members pay via monthly auto-scheduled Mindbody payments;
some per-session; some hybrid.

Prices were set by the CEO and raised a couple of months ago. Many
grandfathered legacy rates make revenue messy.

## Systems and tools

- **Mindbody** — main scheduling CRM and payment intake. Not set up
  properly for tracking; Josie has been cleaning it up (doing well), and the
  cleanup has surfaced member mischarges and missed charges.
- **GoHighLevel (GHL)** — sales/marketing pipeline. Constantly breaking:
  Facebook-ads leads not recorded or not flowing through as they should.
  Run by the CEO today, transitioning to the COO.
- Also: Outlook, GoDaddy, Facebook Business Suite, Google Admin Console;
  P&L from a third-party accountant.
- Trainers use **their own unknown tools** for workout logging and client
  check-ins (apps + texting). All scheduling goes through the business
  Mindbody account; appointments recur weekly far into the future.
- COO ambition: in-house workout tracking + full member profiles visible to
  any trainer and admin staff. (The discovery-meeting intake artifact —
  `discovery-email-generator/` in this repo — is the first step: it replaced
  the CEO's paper form + retyped email with a portal trainers can access.)

## The sales pipeline (CEO-owned, ~90%)

FB ad → form → GHL "opportunity" → outreach → qualifying call → schedule
**discovery meeting** (goals, nutrition, movement, restrictions) → perform
the meeting → follow-up → hand-off to trainer. No real walk-ins: the
discovery meeting is required before any workouts. The meeting is highly
valuable but takes too long (COO working on shortening it). The CEO also
answers the main phone because leads need context the front desk doesn't
have — an empowerment gap that is part tooling (CEO holds GHL/lead control)
and part training.

## Known pain points

**Business:** metrics (CAC, LTV, retention, churn) are convoluted or
impossible to retrieve quickly; churn is guessed at 3–7 months of membership
duration with no data behind it; nobody tracks about-to-lapse members.
Ad-spend ROI is unknowable because the systems don't talk to each other.

**Members:** payment surprises from the Mindbody cleanup (mischarges /
missed charges); communication gaps when a member asks someone without
context on their situation; service is good in-building, lacking outside it.

**Manual grind:** lead nurture/follow-up (partly automated in GHL but needs
heavy human-in-the-loop), client-maintenance texting (trainers), social
posting (Josie manages, CEO is the on-camera figurehead), report building,
and nutrition guides that take multiple manual hours each. Payroll
mechanics for the trainer split are unknown to the COO — a CEO interview
question.

## What works and must be protected

- Team cohesion and shared values, even across the 1099 relationship.
- The prescriptive methodology — proven product-market fit; workouts are
  individualized with true measurements and quantifiable metrics.
- The discovery meeting (when leads get booked).
- Strong member loyalty.

## Standards and consistency

Trainer comp is 55% of the member's session rate. No client hoarding — the
culture is genuinely good — but the contractor model makes a uniform
standard hard to enforce. 1-on-1s mostly follow the Momentum methodology
(a few trainers take liberties). **Physics 101 is the inconsistency
hotspot**: trainers run the class their own way, so classes aren't
interchangeable and members follow a favorite trainer instead of flowing
between classes.

The COO's margin hunches: pricing too low; the 55% split too generous on
business-generated leads (the business pays 100% of acquisition cost, then
55% of fulfillment); the midday dead zone; unmeasurable ad spend.

## Directives for the question sets

- **Four questionnaires:** Trainer, Front Desk, CEO, and Josie (her own,
  covering all three hats: trainer / admin & Mindbody / social content).
- **Never ask trainers about compensation.** Comp topics live only in the
  CEO set.
- CEO set: pure information-gleaning, nothing that puts him on the
  defensive. Extract: vision; specific things he knows he shouldn't be
  doing himself; what takes too much time; what he'd like to do in the
  future; problems the business solves and should solve; his elevator pitch
  (offer example framings to react to); payroll mechanics; the history/why
  behind pricing and comp decisions.
- Trainer set: tools they actually use; what takes too much time; anything
  that feels pointless or unexplained; midday dead-zone ideas; what they'd
  standardize in Physics 101; discovery-handoff quality; unpaid admin time;
  new-facility client migration estimate.
- Front desk set: short (~10 minutes), warm and low-pressure, a mix of
  multiple-choice and open-ended; hospitality observations and what she'd
  need to take on more.
- Length targets: front desk ~10 min; trainers 15–20 min; CEO up to an hour.
- Name field shown but optional on all questionnaires.
- Everyone completes it on the main front-desk computer via the artifact
  link (draft protection matters — shared device).
